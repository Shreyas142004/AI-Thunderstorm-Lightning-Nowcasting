System Overview & Mission

What is AeroCast AI?

AeroCast AI is an intelligent, physics-informed Convective Weather and Thunderstorm Nowcasting System built specifically for Karnataka and the Western Ghats. Conventional weather forecast models (like GFS and ECMWF) update only once every 3 to 6 hours on coarse grid resolutions (10 to 25 km), completely missing rapid tropical convective cells that form, explode, and strike within 30 to 60 minutes.

AeroCast AI bridges this critical gap by ingesting live surface AWS telemetry, calculating 62 domain-specific thermodynamic indices (like Dew Point Depression and Vapor Pressure Deficit), running a calibrated Soft-Voting GBDT Ensemble (LightGBM, XGBoost, HistGradientBoosting) trained on 27 years of Karnataka climatology, and employing Google Gemini Generative AI to produce plain-English sector advisories for plantation farmers, highway commuters, and outdoor safety.

🔬 Core Atmospheric Parameters Explained

Discover how each meteorological variable is measured, why it governs convective storm physics, and how AeroCast AI calculates them.

💧

1. Relative Humidity (RH)

Measured in Percentage (%)
Moisture Metric

Relative Humidity (RH) is the ratio of water vapor present in the air compared to the maximum amount of water vapor the air can hold at that specific temperature:

Definition Formulation RH = (Actual Vapor Pressure / Saturation Vapor Pressure) × 100%
  • Role in Thunderstorms: High humidity provides the latent heat energy required to sustain powerful convective updrafts once warm air begins rising.
  • The "Nocturnal Humidity Trap": In coastal Karnataka (Puttur, Mangalore, Udupi), nighttime radiative cooling drops temperature near the dew point, driving RH above 90% to 98% on calm, clear nights.
⚠️ Why standard AI fails: Basic ML models confuse 95% nighttime humidity with storms, producing false alarms. AeroCast AI combines RH with diurnal solar heating (Df) and DPD to eliminate nocturnal false alarms.
🌡️

2. Dew Point & DPD

Measured in Degrees Celsius (°C)
Saturation Metric

The Dew Point (Td) is the exact temperature to which air must be cooled at constant pressure to reach 100% saturation, causing water vapor to condense into liquid dew or cloud droplets.

Dew Point Depression Formulation DPD = T (Dry Bulb Temp) − Td (Dew Point)
  • Low DPD (< 3.0°C): Indicates the boundary-layer air is nearly saturated. Cloud base height (LCL) lowers significantly.
  • High Dew Point (≥ 22°C–25°C): Signals enormous moisture mass in the lower atmosphere, providing high-octane fuel for explosive tropical cumulonimbus development.
💡 Convective Signature: When high surface temperature (> 28°C) coincides with low DPD (< 3.5°C), the atmosphere has maximum convective available potential energy.
🌫️

3. Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)

Measured in Hectopascals (hPa)
Evaporative Demand

Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) is the difference between the pressure exerted by water vapor in completely saturated air (es) and the actual water vapor pressure (e):

Magnus-Tetens Thermodynamic Equation VPD = es(T) − e(Td) es = 6.112 × exp[ (17.67 × T) / (T + 243.5) ]
  • Low VPD (≤ 0.8 to 1.2 hPa): Air cannot absorb additional moisture. Condensation is triggered, accelerating cloud droplet coalescence and rainfall.
  • High VPD (> 2.5 hPa): Air is very dry. Raindrops falling from cloud bases evaporate before reaching the ground (virga effect), suppressing storms.
🔬 Why VPD is superior to RH: Relative humidity changes non-linearly with temperature, whereas VPD provides a direct, absolute physical measure of the atmospheric saturation gap.
📉

4. Barometric Pressure Drop (ΔP)

Measured in Hectopascals (hPa)
Kinematic Trigger

Barometric Pressure Drop (ΔP) tracks the rapid change in atmospheric surface pressure over short intervals (1-hour or 24-hour rate of change):

Pressure Tendency Formulation ΔP = Pbaseline − Pcurrent (hPa)
  • Why Pressure Drops Before Storms: Developing thunderstorms act as intense thermal chimneys. Buoyant, warm air rapidly evacuates upward, creating a localized low-pressure surface vacuum.
  • Threshold Indicator (≥ 1.5 to 3.0 hPa): A sudden pressure drop of 1.5 hPa or more strongly signals incoming convective convergence, gust fronts, and lightning activity.
⚡ The Squall Line Indicator: Just before a severe convective downburst strikes, pressure reaches a sharp minimum before a rapid "pressure jump" caused by the rain-cooled cold pool downdraft.

📍 Inside Karnataka Queries

Queries for any village (e.g., Puttur, Nekkilady, 108 Kalthur, Bantwal, Bengaluru) instantly resolve to verified coordinates, run 62-feature physics calculations, stream S-band Doppler radar sweeps, and display +30m, +60m, and +90m timeline predictions.

🛡️ Outside Karnataka Geofence Lock

Queries for places outside Karnataka (e.g., Goa, Sikkim, Andhra Pradesh, Mumbai) are identified and isolated. The map flies to their true geographic location, but nowcast predictions and weather cards are locked to prevent misleading non-Karnataka weather inferences.
